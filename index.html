    §02 Atlas

    …But where were they taken from?

    The thirty objects left their homes in many ways: seized in wars and punitive expeditions, carried off after conquests, divided after excavations, bought from people who had little choice, smuggled, or given as gifts that later changed hands.

    Most of the routes end in a few capitals: London, Paris and Berlin above all. Many objects were taken where European powers ruled or dominated, from Benin City in 1897 to Beijing in 1860 and 1900. Others moved between European states at war, like the Horses of Saint Mark or the Codex Gigas.

    Putting them side by side shows who was able to take, who has been able to keep, and who has managed to get objects back: the dotted routes have since been reversed.

    Every object page says how the object left, who claims it and who holds it, with the sources for each position.

    §04 Why this matters

    Our virtual tours aim to educate, provoke thought, and foster dialogue about the lasting impacts of colonialism on cultural heritage.

    By digitally reuniting these objects with their stories, we hope to contribute to the ongoing conversation about repatriation and cultural preservation.
